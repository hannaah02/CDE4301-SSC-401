Recap

This subsection focusses on effectively transfering heat from the receiver to the thermal energy storage (TES) with minimal power consumption. In the interim, water was selected at heat transfer fluid, pump-driven thermal transmission method was validated and heat loss was identified as limitation with PUR/PIR selected as optimal material.

In this subsection, the implementation and evaluation of insulation and thermal transmission methods are conducted, analysing their impact on the overall system performance.



Insulation

state clearly how its made. details.

The PUR/PIR insulation selected in the interim is compared with the commercially available aircon insulation (polypropylene random polymer, PPR), and a translucent insulation based off the concept of transparent insulation material (TIM).

PUR/PIR insulation was selected in the interim, comparing the cost, thickness required and durability. For this experiment, spray PUR/PIR foam is used with a 7mm diameter aluminium duct to encapsulate the insulation.

Aircon insulation is a common insulation that is evaluated due to its ease of implementation. Here, PPR of thickness 5mm and inner diameter 17mm is used.

TIM prevents heat loss thorugh prevention of convection in the air pockets (see below), while allowing irradiance to heat the absorber (hose with water) though the transparent material. Using this concept, translucent corrugated plastic board is used to wrap around the hose to allow transmission of irradiance while trapping air between the layers.

Insulation Experiment

purpose of this, and how it fits with the system as a whole

The insulation experiments were conducted under different conditions to assess their rate of heat loss and was repeated for validation. As the system is expected to function at high temperatures up to 60ºC, and with 40ºC as lowest temperature, the trends at high temperatures from 40-60ºC is the focus with the higher ranges of 60ºc being most important.

In shade

Under shaded conditions, PUR/PIR and aircon insulation performed best with similar heat loss rates at 60ºC, with PUR/PIR having lower rate of heat loss than aircon insulation as temperature drops below 60ºC.

Under indirect sunlight

Under indirect sunlight, PUR/PIR insulation performs best due to its lower thermal conductivity. This trend is further supported by the transition test below, where PUR/PIR is also observed to maintain temperature over 40ºC for longest duration.

Under direct sunlight

Under direct sunlight, aircon insulation has the lowest net heat loss. This results from its higher absorptivity, allowing it to absorb more solar energy. The direct sunlight to shade test below verifies this, with aircon insulation also able to keep the water over 40ºC the longest.

Analysis

Under sunlight, the varation in performance is attributed to differences in absorptivity. The black aircon insulation has a high abrorptivity (0.9 [2]) whereas the aluminium covered PUR/PIR insulation has a significantly lower absorptivity (0.15 [3]). This makes the aircon insulation more sensitive to irradiance, as seen by the temperature fluctuations below. Under direct sunlight, higher irradiance allows the aircon insulation to absorb greater amounts of energy, resulting in improved overall performance.

However, in the intended application, the insulation will be largely underneath the reflectors (see below) and will be shaded. Under these conditions, the PUR/PIR insulation demonstrates better thermal performance.



Integration of system

Initial integration

The pipe is routed from the receiver through the central frame and connected to the TES (20L of water) and pump (see below). A longer pipe length was used initially to allow TES movement, and is reduced later.

Insulation was not applied along the full pipe length due to flexibility limitations of PUR/PIR and restrictions on insulating certain sections of pipe to avoid damage on removal.

Below are the temperature of water in the TES for the uninsulated and insulated system.

The temperature difference between TES and control water temperature increased from 5.25ºC to 8.25ºC (57.14% improvement).

Isolation of the TES from the receiver

After sundown, TES temperature dropped to match the control, indicating complete loss of stored thermal energy overnight. This is attributed to the continuous pump operation, causing heat loss thorugh the receiver. To reduce heat loss, the TES is isolated from the receiver system between 5pm and 10am through:

  1. the installation of a relay to shut off power to the pump between 5pm to 10am
  2. lifting the TES inlet pipe from the surface of the water in the TES (physically isolating system)

The volume of water in the TES was also reduced to 10L to increase maximum temperature and extend the duration the water is kept over 40ºC. The results of these changes are below.

Based on the results, the TES water temperature is consistently above the control water temperature overnight, indicating retained thermal energy carried forward from the previous day. The effect of this is seen in the rise in the temperature difference from 8.25ºC to 11.56ºC (40.12% improvement).

Shortening of the pipe and improved insulation

Thermal losses due to pipe length and incomplete insulation were reduced by repositioning the TES at the centre of the rig and improving insulation coverings by including aircon insulation at areas the PUR/PIR insulation is unable to cover.

On removal, it was observed that some sections of the pipe lacked the PUR/PIR insulation along longer pipe sections due to spray insulation being unable to reach deeper into the pipe.

Also, support structures to maintain central position of hose is unable to be inserted in the long sections of pipe, resulting in the hose laying on the edge of the aluminium pipe (see below).

Due to these, the results were less efficient than expected. The updated insulations removes the need for long pipe lengths and the issues associated with it.

The results of the changes are as follows:

The temperature difference increased from 11.56ºC to 18.88ºC, which is a 63.32% improvement. The water temperature is also kept above 40ºC for about 9 hours consistently.

Conclusion and Further Work

The integrated system successfully concentrates and stores thermal energy and maintains water above 40ºC for 9 hours.

Although PUR/PIR provides better thermal performance, its rigidity and integration issues makes it difficult to implement effectively. Aircon insulation is thus preferred due its flexibility and good performance.

Also, future improvements includes the testing of thermosyphons in the prototype CSP rig, which will be explained in the next section.



Testing methods of thermal transmission

In the interim, pump-driven circulation was validated as method of thermal transmission. In this section, thermosyphons are evaluated at small-scale to assess their feasibility as a passive alternative.

Baseline test of standard thermosyphon

A standard thermosyphon configuration was constucted to establish a baseline (see diagram below).

Clear convective circulation patterns can be observed at the outlet (top pipe), confirming working of the thermosyphon.

Horizontal configuration test

The setup is repeated with the pipes arranged horizontally, with a 0m height difference between the tank (inlet and outlet pipe) and the heated section (see below).

No clear convection patterns was observed in this configuration. This confirms that the operation is dependant on gravittaional effects and density differences. While heat transfer still occurs through conduction and local convection, natural convection as in the baseline test does not occur, which limits the effectiveness of the heat transfer.

Test with constraints as in integrated system

The third test is conducted with the heated section below the tank (as in baseline test), but replicates the constraints of the integrated system, where drilling the sides of the container would affect its insulation. The heated section was also replaced with a coil to simulate the receiver. The rough layout is in the diagram below.

Flow was observed at the outlet. The onset of flow was delayed due to the longer pipe and complex layouts. Overall, it demonstrates that flow can be achieved despite increased complexity, so long as height differences between the pipe inlet, pipe outlet and the heated section is maintained.

Future work

Further testings is required to assess the thermosyphon's feasibility in the prototype. A thermosyphon is not self-priming and requires regular monitoring to prevent air accumulation. Furthermore, the heat loss through the receiver after sundown needs to be addressed thorugh addition of valves at the inlet and outlet pipes to isolate the TES from the receiver.

The implementation also requires the TES to be located outside of the 1x1m rig, exceeding the intended footprint of the system. Hence, future work includes exploring alternate TES configuurations to maintain height difference within the TES and receiver and maintain 1x1m configuration.



Appendix XX: Design specifications for thermal transmission

Power consumption per rig is estimated against power available per meter square for solar panels, and 1288m^2 of rigs available, and 1176m^2 of area available for solar panel (front of hotel rooftop). Total power produced by solar panels is 176,400W (assuming minimum produced of 150W/m^2). [https://www.energydawnice.com/solar-panel-output-per-square-meter/] Hence, maxiumum power that can be consumed by each rig assuming full space usage is 136.96W. Thus, the power consumption per rig is set at ≤136.96W.

The efficiency of the system's insulation is measured by:

  1. Heat loss per meter
  2. Pipe losses as percentage of total energy delivered, and

Heat loss per meter is the main measurement of the insulation's effectiveness. In residential units, the heat loss per meter is within the range of 5.1-10.8W/m for insulated domestic hot water systems. [https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355301258_Heat_Loss_Due_to_Domestic_Hot_Water_Pipes] For our system usage, the representative threshold value of 7.95W/m will be used in design specifications.

Pipe losses as percentage of total energy delivered is on average 16-19% in residential units. [https://www1.eere.energy.gov/buildings/publications/pdfs/building_america/hot_water_distribution_model.pdf] In conventional hot water systems, pipe losses are expressed as a percentage of the total energy input to the water heater. However, in the CSP system, there is no heater supplying energy as all the energy is collected from solar energy. In the prototype, the total energy delivered is defined as the total thermal energy that the water absorbs at the receiver. Hence, we set the value in the design specifications as 17.5% for reference which is the average estimate for pipe losses based on residential hot water systems.

In shade

The PUR/PIR and aircon insulation performed best at the start with the same low rate of heat loss, but as temperature drops, the aircon insulation graph has a higher rate of heat loss than PUR/PIR insulation (see below). The translucent insulation did not perform as well as the PUR/PIR and aircon insulation at high temperatures (around 60ºC), but as the temperature fell, the rate of heat loss improved, reaching lower heat loss than both PUR/PIR and aircon insulation (see below).

Overall, the CSP system is expected to function at high temperatures above 40ºC up to 60ºC. Thus, the trend at high temperatures is most important, of which PUR/PIR insulation performed best, with aircon insulation being second best.

In sunlight

When conducting the experiments under sunlight, 2 different results were obtained where PUR/PIR and aircon insulation performed best in each of them. This is determined to be the result of testing under different conditions: direct and indirect sunlight. When conducted under mainly direct sunlight, the aircon insulation performed better with a lower rate of heat loss, whereas in mainly indirect sunlight, the PUR/PIR insulation performed better. This is theorised to be a result of the different absorptivity between the aircon insulation and the PUR/PIR insulation. As the aircon insulation is of black colour, it has a high absorptivity of about 0.9 [2], whereas the aluminium foil covering the PUR/PIR insulation has an absorptivity of 0.15. [3] Between the two insulations, PUR/PIR insulation will thus have lower energy absorbed than the aircon insulation. This is verified as the PUR/PIR has more stable temperature under sunlight compared to the aircon insulation (see below). This accounts for the variations between the aircon and PUR/PIR insulation results conducted under sunlight.

Under direct sunlight, there is higher irradiance compared to indirect. So, the aircon insulation is able to absorb larger amount of solar energy, which offsets the heat loss causing the lower net rate of heat loss oberved under that condition. As such, aircon insulation performing better under direct sunlight.
