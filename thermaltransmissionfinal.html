Insulation

In the interim, PUR/PIR insulation was the best option. In this section, we compare the PUR/PIR insulation with commonly used airconditioning insulation (material: XX), and translucent insulating material (TIM)-style insulation. These insulations and its usage in the tests are explained below.

PUR/PIR insulation is selected in the interim. For the project, we use spray PUR/PIR foam with a Aluminium duct (7mm diameter) to encapsulate the insulation.

Airconditioning insulation is a common material and can be bought off the shelves, reducing manufacturing time. We use XX type of airconditioning insulation.

TIM prevents heat loss and allows irradiance to heat the absorber (pipe with HTF). The transparent insulation (allowing transmission of irradiance) consists of pockets of stagnant air by design, which improves thermal resistance by eliminating convection. Using the concepts of TIM, (single layer) translucent corrugated plastic board is used to wrap around the absorber (hose) to allow transmission and reduce convection of air.

The insulations are tested in shade, under sunlight, and under sunlight moving to shade, with control (no insulation) and repeated 3 times for accuracy. The results are below.

In the shade, the PUR/PIR performed marginally better than airconditioning insulation. In the sunlight, the PUR/PIR performed best. Notably, the TIM was able to achieve a higher equilibrium temperature than PUR/PIR and airconditioning insulation under sunlight, which can be accounted by transmission heating the absorber or by conduction or radiation based on material properties. In the experiment starting in sunlight and ending in shade, the PUR/PIR performed best. Overall, PUR/PIR performed best in all experiments.

Testing methods of thermal transmission

In the interim, pumps have been proven to allow thermal transmission. In this section, we assess thermosyphons in the system, assuming that the TES is able to be raised above the receiver.

In small scale tests, we assessed if flow is observed in standard thermosyphon methods and if a removal of height difference allowed flow. The tests indicate that the height difference of 0m was unable to allow flow, which can be observed in standard thermosyphons as comparison and control. As such, to use passive methods, height difference is necessary.

However, in our TES, the pipe are unable to be laid out exactly as the standard thermosyphon small scale tests. Hence, we test a small scale system using the limitations of our prototype rig (namely, hose fed through top of TES and heated section as coil).
