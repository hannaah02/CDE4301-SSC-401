5.1 Comparison between the designed CSP system and existing small-scale CSP systems

A research paper published by Gazi University presents a small-scale CSP system with a reflector area of 3.6 m² using a parabolic trough configuration and solar tracking []. The system uses water as the heat transfer fluid but does not incorporate thermal energy storage. While the study does not specify a particular application, this design is not well suited for hotel hot water systems. Hotels require a continuous and large supply of hot water, meaning multiple units or a significantly larger collector would be needed. However, deploying multiple parabolic troughs introduces spacing requirements to prevent mutual shading, which reduces effective use of available installation area. Alternatively, installing a single large unit on a rooftop may negatively impact the building’s aesthetics, which is an important consideration for hotel operators.

Another study published by Taylor & Francis describes a 1.5 m² parabolic trough collector designed for domestic water heating in Pakistan []. The system was able to heat 100 L of water to 60 °C within 5 hours, although the number of collectors used was not specified. Similar to the previous design, water is used as the working fluid, and circulation is achieved through a thermosiphon mechanism. However, this system also doesn’t include thermal energy storage, which is a critical limitation for the intended application, as the proposed system must be capable of supplying hot water beyond daylight hours.
