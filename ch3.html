3.5 Integrated Test Results

This section evaluates the performance on integration of the light, thermal transmission and TES subsections.

Initial integration

The pipe is routed from the receiver through the central frame and connected to the TES (20L of water) and pump (see below). A longer pipe length was used initially to allow flexibility in positioning TES, and is reduced later.

Insulation was not applied along the full pipe length due to flexibility limitations of PUR/PIR and restrictions on insulating certain sections of pipe to avoid damage on removal.

The temperature difference between TES and control water temperature increased from 5.25ºC to 8.25ºC (57.14% improvement).

Isolation of the TES from the receiver

After sundown, TES temperature dropped to match the control, indicating complete loss of stored thermal energy overnight. This is attributed to the continuous pump operation, causing heat loss thorugh the receiver. To reduce heat loss, the TES is isolated from the receiver system between 5pm and 10am through:

  1. the installation of a relay to shut off power to the pump between 5pm to 10am
  2. lifting the TES inlet pipe from the surface of the water in the TES (physically isolating system)

The volume of water in the TES was also reduced to 10L to increase maximum temperature and extend the duration the water is kept over 40ºC. The results of these changes are below.

Based on the results, the TES water temperature is consistently above the control water temperature overnight, indicating retained thermal energy carried forward from the previous day, as temperature difference increased from 8.25ºC to 11.56ºC (40.12% improvement). Thus, the system's abilty to retain temperature over 40ºC is lengthened.

Shortening of the pipe and improved insulation

The TES was repositioned to shorten the pipe. This reduces total surface area available for heat exchange with the environment, thus, reducing heat losses along the pipes. Insulation coverage was improved by including aircon insulation at areas the PUR/PIR insulation is unable to cover.

On removal, it was observed that some sections of the pipe lacked the PUR/PIR insulation along longer pipe sections due to spray insulation being unable to reach deeper into the pipe.

Also, support structures to maintain central position of hose is unable to be inserted in the long sections of pipe, resulting in the hose laying on the edge of the aluminium pipe (see below).

Due to these, the results were less efficient than expected. The updated insulations removes the need for long pipe lengths and the issues associated with it.

The temperature difference increased from 11.56ºC to 18.88ºC, which is a 63.32% improvement. Water temperature was sustained above 40ºC for approximately 9 hours, demonstrating system performance for sustained thermal storage.

Conclusion and Further Work

The integrated system maintains water temperatures above 40°C for approximately 9 hours, demonstrating effective thermal storage and transmission performance.

Although PUR/PIR provides better thermal performance, its rigidity and integration issues makes it difficult to implement effectively. Aircon insulation is thus preferred due its flexibility, ease of implementation, and good thermal properties.
