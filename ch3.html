3.5 Integrated Test Results

This section evaluates the performance on integration of the light, thermal transmission and TES subsections.

3.5.1 Initial integration

The pipe is routed from the receiver through the central frame and connected to the TES and pump. A longer pipe length was used initially to allow flexibility in positioning TES, and is reduced later. Control plastic drum with minimal insulation with equal amount of water volume is used in comparison with insulated TES.

Pipe is not insulated initially. Later insulation was partially applied along the full pipe length due to flexibility limitations of PUR/PIR and restrictions on insulating certain sections of pipe to avoid damage on removal. In the end, pipes are fully insulated.

Sensor used: DS18B20, RDXL6SD-US-B-CAL-3, SP-420, DHT11(for rain detection)

3.5.2.1 Iteration 1: Effect of Pipe Insulation
Purpose: To assess the impact of pipe insulation on the gradient of TES outlet water temperature and the maximum temperature difference between TES outlet water and control water.
Method: Two experimental conditions were compared: (1) without pipe insulation (14/3–15/3) and (2) with partial pipe insulation (16/3–17/3). In both cases, water was pumped continuously at 20% of the maximum pump capacity, the TES was filled with 20 L of water, and 25 PCM pipes (maximum number of pipes) were installed inside the TES.
Hypothesis: The insulated system will produce a greater maximum temperature difference between TES water and control water, and the temperature in the TES will increase faster than in the uninsulated system.

Discussion: The insulated system achieved a higher TES water temperature and a larger TES–control temperature difference than the uninsulated system. This is because pipe insulation reduces heat loss from the fluid during circulation, allowing more thermal energy collected from the receiver to be retained and transferred into the TES. As a result, the maximum energy stored in the TES increases, leading to a higher peak temperature. In addition, the rate of temperature rise in the TES is steeper in the insulated case, indicating faster heat accumulation. There is also a longer temperature retention at peak temperature of insulated system compared to uninsulated one. This trend is consistent with the expected reduction in heat loss along the piping network (see Appendix B0 for theoretical computation).

Result: The maximum temperature difference between TES water and control water increased from 5.19°C in the uninsulated system to 8 °C in the insulated system, corresponding to an improvement of 54%. The average heating slope of the TES water also increased from 2.03°C/h to 2.33 °C/h and the duration of staying at max temperature is extended in insulated system. Therefore, the results support the hypothesis. At the 80% confidence, two-tailed test maximum temperature difference: p ≈ 0.148 and heating slope: p ≈ 0.200 show both are statistically significant

Limitation: Solar irradiance varied from day to day, so the two cases were not tested under perfectly identical weather conditions. The analysis assumes that the irradiance conditions were broadly comparable based on similar peak and average values, although 15/3 was a rainy day and may not be suitable for direct comparison in hypothesis testing. Other uncontrolled factors, such as ambient temperature and transient cloud cover, may also affect the observed temperature profiles.

After sundown, TES temperature dropped to match the control, indicating complete loss of stored thermal energy overnight. This is attributed to the continuous pump operation, causing heat loss thorugh the receiver. To reduce heat loss, the TES is isolated from the receiver system between 5pm and 10am through:

  1. the installation of a relay to shut off power to the pump between 5pm to 10am
  2. lifting the TES inlet pipe from the surface of the water in the TES (physically isolating system)

The volume of water in the TES was also reduced to 10L to increase maximum temperature and extend the duration the water is kept over 40ºC. The results of these changes are below.

Based on the results, the TES water temperature is consistently above the control water temperature overnight, indicating retained thermal energy carried forward from the previous day, as temperature difference increased from 8.25ºC to 11.56ºC (40.12% improvement). Thus, the system's abilty to retain temperature over 40ºC is lengthened.

Shortening of the pipe and improved insulation

The TES was repositioned to shorten the pipe. This reduces total surface area available for heat exchange with the environment, thus, reducing heat losses along the pipes. Insulation coverage was improved by including aircon insulation at areas the PUR/PIR insulation is unable to cover.

On removal, it was observed that some sections of the pipe lacked the PUR/PIR insulation along longer pipe sections due to spray insulation being unable to reach deeper into the pipe.

Also, support structures to maintain central position of hose is unable to be inserted in the long sections of pipe, resulting in the hose laying on the edge of the aluminium pipe (see below).

Due to these, the results were less efficient than expected. The updated insulations removes the need for long pipe lengths and the issues associated with it.

Conclusion and Further Work

The integrated system maintains water temperatures above 40°C for approximately 9 hours, demonstrating effective thermal storage and transmission performance.

Although PUR/PIR provides better thermal performance, its rigidity and integration issues makes it difficult to implement effectively. Aircon insulation is thus preferred due its flexibility, ease of implementation, and good thermal properties.
